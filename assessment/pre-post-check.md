# Pre/Post Check

Status: draft. Optional unless the course carries credit or a certificate (see
[COURSE-BRIEF.md](../COURSE-BRIEF.md), open question 4).

The same short check after the tool check in the opening (5 minutes, taken from
the parking-lot discussion) and again during the closing feedback (slide 87).
Not graded; tell learners that. Keep it anonymous, or pair pre and post with a
self-chosen code word.

## Questions

| # | Outcome | Question | Answer (instructor only) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | LO1 | An AI tool gives you a confident answer with a statistic. True or false: because it sounds confident, it has checked the fact. | False. It predicts likely words; it doesn't look things up |
| 2 | LO2 | Rewrite this prompt to get a better result: "Write a meeting agenda." | Credit for adding at least three of: role, context, task detail, format, constraints |
| 3 | LO3 | Name two things you'd check before sending an AI-written email to a client. | Any two of: accuracy, completeness, fairness, fit for purpose |
| 4 | LO3 | Your first result is too long and too formal. What's your next step? | Follow up in the same chat with a specific change, e.g. "Shorter and friendlier" |
| 5 | LO4 | Which of these is safe to paste into a free AI tool? (a) a customer's email with their address (b) a public job posting (c) a confidential contract | (b) |

**Confidence, before and after:** "How confident are you that you could write
a prompt that gets a useful result for a real work task?" 1 (not at all) to 5
(very).
