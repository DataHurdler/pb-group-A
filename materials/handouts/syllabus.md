## About this workshop

AI tools like ChatGPT, Claude, and Copilot can draft, summarize, and organize
work in seconds, but only if you ask well. This hands-on workshop teaches you
how to write clear requests (called *prompts*), check what comes back, and use
AI safely with work information. You'll spend most of the day practicing, and
you'll leave with a tested prompt for a real task from your own job.

**Who it's for:** professionals new to AI tools, from complete beginners to
occasional users. **Prerequisites:** none. No coding or technical background
needed.

## By the end of the day, you will be able to

1. **Explain** in plain words how AI chatbots produce answers, and what they do well and badly.
2. **Write** clear, structured prompts using five parts: role, context, task, format, and constraints.
3. **Evaluate** AI outputs for accuracy, completeness, fairness, and fit.
4. **Refine** prompts using examples, step-by-step requests, and follow-up messages.
5. **Decide** what information is safe to put into an AI tool, and remove what isn't.
6. **Build** a reusable prompt for one real task from your own job.

## What to bring and set up

- A laptop with a web browser and its charger.
- A free account on at least one of ChatGPT, Claude, or Microsoft Copilot,
  tested before the day. If you're using a work laptop, check that it can open
  the tool; some workplaces block AI sites.
- One task you do every week that involves writing, summarizing, or organizing
  information. You'll build your capstone prompt around it.
- **No confidential documents.** We use made-up details all day.

## Modules

**1. How AI tools work.** What a large language model (LLM) is, and why it can
sound confident and still be wrong. You'll test what AI does well and where it
struggles.

**2. Anatomy of a good prompt.** The five parts of a clear request. You'll fix
weak prompts and write the first draft of a prompt for your own task.

**3. Techniques.** Three ways to get better results: showing examples, asking
for step-by-step work, and improving answers with follow-up messages.

**4. Checking outputs.** How to spot made-up facts (*hallucinations*) and
biased wording, and a four-question check to use before you rely on any
output. Includes a team prompt bake-off.

**5. Using AI responsibly.** What happens to what you type, what's safe to
share, and how to remove sensitive details while keeping your prompt useful.

**6. Capstone.** How to reuse a good prompt so it saves time without losing
quality. Then you finish your own prompt, test it, and present it to your table.

## How the day works

Every module follows the same pattern: a short explanation (15 minutes or
less), a live demonstration, hands-on practice, then sharing and questions.
You'll work alone, in pairs, and in small groups. Questions are welcome at any
time, and nobody is graded.

**What you'll produce:**

- A first-draft prompt for your own task (Module 2), improved over three rounds (Module 3)
- A team prompt for the bake-off (Module 4)
- **Your capstone:** a tested, reusable prompt for a real task from your job,
  checked for accuracy and privacy, and presented in three minutes to your table
  (Module 6)
- A one-week action plan for using it

## Responsible use

- **Data privacy.** Don't put personal or confidential information into AI
  tools: names with details, contact information, health or pay information,
  contracts, or anything your employer hasn't approved. In this workshop, use
  made-up details only.
- **Your workplace or school rules come first.** Check which tools are approved
  and what you may share before using AI with real work.
- **Academic integrity.** If you are a UNO student, follow each course's rules
  on AI use and UNO's academic integrity expectations. Say when you've used AI
  where that's required.
- **You're responsible for what you send.** Check every AI output before using it.
