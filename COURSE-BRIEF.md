# Course Brief

Status: draft

## 1. The course

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Subject** | AI and LLM prompt engineering for workplace tasks |
| **Format** | One day, in person, 8:30–4:30 |
| **Instructional time** | 6.5 hours (390 minutes), plus a 60-minute lunch and two 15-minute breaks |
| **Audience** | Novice to entry-level professionals with little or no experience of AI tools. No coding background assumed |
| **Class size** | 15–25 |
| **Tools** | ChatGPT, Claude, or Copilot. Free versions are enough for everything |
| **Tone** | Plain, practical, encouraging, low-pressure. Learners are assumed to be slightly intimidated |

## 2. Learning outcomes

By the end of the day, learners can:

| ID | Outcome | Taught in | Checked by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LO1 | Explain in plain words how an AI chatbot produces an answer, and name what it does well and badly | Module 1 | Exercises 1–2 |
| LO2 | Write clear, structured prompts using role, context, task, format, and constraints | Modules 2–3 | Exercises 3–5 |
| LO3 | Evaluate outputs for accuracy, completeness, fairness, and fit, and refine prompts to improve them | Modules 3–4 | Exercises 6–8 |
| LO4 | Decide what is safe to put into an AI tool and remove sensitive details | Module 5 | Exercises 9–10 |
| LO5 | Apply prompting to one real task from their own job, producing a reusable prompt | Modules 2–6 | Exercises 4, 6, 11, 12 |

## 3. Assumptions

Change any of these and adjust [schedule.md](schedule.md).

- **Lunch is 60 minutes**, which puts the day at 8:30–4:30. A 45-minute lunch
  would end the day at 4:15.
- **Learners bring their own laptops** with a working browser and can reach at
  least one of the three AI tools.
- **Groups of 4–5 sit at tables** for the bake-off, the traffic-light
  exercise, and capstone presentations. With 15–25 learners that's 4–5 tables.
- **Capstone presentations happen at tables**, not to the whole room. Twenty-five
  individual presentations would take over an hour; tables let everyone
  present in 15 minutes. Three nominated capstones go to the whole room in the
  closing.
- **Module 5 is 45 minutes** and the others 60, so the day fits 6.5 hours with
  an opening and a closing.

## 4. Open questions

| # | Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Date, venue, and instructor? | Fills the title slide, agenda, and pre-course email |
| 2 | Do learners' employers block any of the three tools on work laptops? | Setup failures are the biggest risk to the first hour |
| 3 | Will learners have work accounts with data protections, or personal free accounts? | Changes how strongly Module 5 warns about pasting work content |
| 4 | Is there a certificate or credit attached? | Decides whether the pre/post check is used |
| 5 | Who collects feedback, and how? | Needed for slide 87 |

## 5. Scope

- **In scope:** what LLMs are and aren't; writing structured prompts; examples,
  step-by-step, iteration; checking outputs; privacy and workplace policy; one
  reusable prompt for the learner's own job.
- **Out of scope:** coding, building AI apps or agents, image generation,
  connecting AI to company systems, legal advice on AI policy. Point people
  who ask toward follow-up resources.
